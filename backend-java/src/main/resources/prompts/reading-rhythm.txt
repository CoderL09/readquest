Sense-group standard
1. A sense group is a unit a skilled reader can understand and say in one breath, with a natural pause after it.
   Group by meaning and speech rhythm, not by mechanically separating every grammar constituent.
2. Prefer 2-7 words per group and usually 2-6 groups per sentence. These are guidelines: keep a longer unit when
   splitting it would damage meaning, and keep a short discourse marker as its own group when it carries a pause.
3. Good boundaries usually occur between major clauses; before a subordinate or relative clause; after an
   introductory time, place, condition, or discourse phrase; and around a true parenthetical interruption.
4. Never split any of these cohesive units unless a strongly contrastive context requires it:
   - a determiner/adjective and its noun;
   - an auxiliary, negation, and lexical verb;
   - a phrasal verb and its particle;
   - a preposition and its object;
   - an infinitive marker and its verb;
   - a possessive, number, date, or fixed expression;
   - an honorific, initial, or abbreviation and the name it belongs to (for example, "Mr. Justice Wargrave").
   - a subordinating conjunction and the clause it introduces. Though, Because, Although, If, When, While,
     Since, Unless, and similar clause openers must never be a standalone sense group;
   - a number or quantity and its complete noun phrase. Keep forms such as "three books", "twenty-one days",
     "the first two chapters", and "300 local residents" together.
5. Attach punctuation to the group immediately before it, except opening quotation marks or brackets, which stay
   with the text that follows. A period inside a common abbreviation such as Mr., Mrs., Dr., Prof., St., or U.S.
   is never a sentence boundary by itself.

Lossless text rules
6. The result must be lossless: preserve every word, contraction, capitalization mark, quotation mark, and item of
   punctuation exactly once and in the original order.
7. Every sense group must be an exact contiguous substring of the original sentence. Never paraphrase, correct,
   normalize, insert, delete, or reorder text. The groups, with only their original intervening whitespace restored,
   must reconstruct the original sentence exactly.
8. Before answering, silently verify coverage from the first character to the final punctuation. If uncertain about
   a boundary, prefer fewer, larger groups over a brittle or unnatural split.
